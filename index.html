On the Influence of Adversarial Loss Functions on Standard DCGAN-Based Image Synthesis

Sanskar Kushwah | M.Tech Thesis | NIT Srinagar

Abstract

Generative adversarial networks (GANs) have proved remarkably successful in realistic image synthesis. However, the choice of adversarial loss function remains a key factor influencing image quality, convergence behavior, and training stability. This paper presents a controlled cross-domain and systematic empirical assessment of five widely used adversarial loss functions, namely Standard GAN, Least Squares GAN (LSGAN), Wasserstein GAN (WGAN), Wasserstein GAN with Gradient Penalty (WGAN-GP), and Hinge GAN, on the training dynamics and generative performance of standard Deep Convolutional GAN (DCGAN) architecture while holding the generator and discriminator architectures, optimization hyperparameters, and training schedules constant. The models are evaluated on three representative datasets spanning natural, remote sensing, and medical imaging domains: CIFAR-10, EuroSAT, and CheXpert. Performance is assessed using Fréchet inception distance (FID), mode variance, and training stability analysis. Experimental results show that LSGAN achieves the most consistent optimization and image quality across the evaluated datasets, while WGAN-GP performs best on CIFAR-10. WGAN consistently preserves the highest sample diversity, highlighting a trade-off between image fidelity and diversity.

Keywords

Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), Adversarial Loss Functions, Deep Convolutional GAN (DCGAN), Image Synthesis, Training Stability, Fréchet Inception Distance (FID), Mode Variance, Cross-Domain Evaluation, CIFAR-10, EuroSAT, CheXpert

Introduction

Deep learning has revolutionized image and video processing by utilizing multi-layer neural networks to automatically extract meaningful features from raw data. Deep generative models have emerged as a powerful class of techniques capable of learning from existing data and generating new samples with similar characteristics. Among these models, Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) have gained significant attention due to their remarkable ability to synthesize realistic images.

A GAN consists of two neural networks: a generator, which creates synthetic images from randomly generated input values, and a discriminator, which attempts to distinguish between real images and synthetic images produced by the generator. During training, the generator learns to produce increasingly realistic images, while the discriminator learns to better identify synthetic images.

Main Contributions:

  • Five standard GAN objective functions are compared on an identical DCGAN architecture. This isolates the effect of the objective function on network training, removing architectural and hyperparameter biases.
  • All experiments are performed on three visually distinct datasets (CIFAR-10, EuroSAT, and CheXpert), enabling a comprehensive cross-domain benchmarking of GAN loss functions across natural-image, remote-sensing, and medical-imaging domains.

Key Observations:

  • LSGAN achieves the most consistent optimization and image quality across the evaluated datasets, with smooth convergence and balanced loss evolution.
  • WGAN-GP performs exceptionally well on CIFAR-10 with the best FID score of 58.86, demonstrating strong performance on natural image datasets.
  • WGAN consistently preserves the highest sample diversity across all datasets, highlighting a trade-off between image quality and diversity.
  • Standard GAN consistently exhibits the least stable training, characterized by discriminator dominance, oscillatory behavior, or persistent divergence.
  • Hinge GAN provides competitive performance with lower computational overhead compared to WGAN-GP.

Methodology

Datasets Evaluated:

  • CIFAR-10: 60,000 color images of 32×32 pixels belonging to 10 object classes. A widely used benchmark for image generation models.
  • EuroSAT: 27,000 Sentinel-2 multispectral satellite images of 64×64 pixels belonging to 10 land-cover classes. Represents remote sensing domain.
  • CheXpert: 191,027 frontal chest radiographs annotated for 14 thoracic pathologies. Represents medical imaging domain.

DCGAN Architecture:

  • Generator: Maps 100-dimensional latent vector to 64×64 RGB image using transposed convolutional layers
  • Discriminator: Takes 64×64 RGB image as input and outputs scalar value using strided convolutional layers
  • Training: 100 epochs on CIFAR-10 and CheXpert; 150 epochs on EuroSAT with batch size 64 (128 for CheXpert)
  • Evaluation: FID computed every 10 epochs using 2000 generated samples

Loss Functions Analyzed

1. Standard GAN (Binary Cross-Entropy)

The original GAN objective using binary cross-entropy loss. Simple to implement but suffers from vanishing gradients and unstable training.

2. Least Squares GAN (LSGAN)

Replaces BCE with least-squares objective. Provides smoother gradients, encourages better distribution matching, and results in more stable training.

3. Wasserstein GAN (WGAN)

Uses Wasserstein-1 distance (Earth Mover's distance) instead of JS divergence. Provides more stable training and meaningful learning signals.

4. WGAN with Gradient Penalty (WGAN-GP)

Replaces weight clipping with differentiable gradient penalty. Preserves critic expressiveness while enforcing Lipschitz constraint, enabling superior stability.

5. Hinge Loss GAN

Adopts SVM-inspired hinge loss with margin-based criterion. Provides stable optimization and effective gradient propagation for both generator and discriminator.

Evaluation Metrics

  • Fréchet Inception Distance (FID): Measures distance between feature distributions of real and generated images. Lower is better.
  • Mode Variance: Measures diversity of generated samples in feature space. Higher indicates greater diversity.
  • Training Stability: Analyzed through generator and discriminator loss trajectories over training epochs.

Conclusions

This comprehensive cross-domain evaluation provides practical guidance for selecting adversarial loss functions for diverse image generation tasks:

  • For consistent performance: Choose LSGAN for stable, reliable image quality across different domains.
  • For natural images: WGAN-GP achieves excellent FID scores on CIFAR-10.
  • For diversity: WGAN preserves the highest sample diversity across all datasets.
  • For efficiency: Hinge GAN provides competitive performance with lower computational overhead.
  • The findings highlight fundamental trade-offs between image quality, sample diversity, and training stability that practitioners must consider when selecting loss functions for their applications.
